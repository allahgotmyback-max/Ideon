Okay... but
how do I actually start?

Before you buy your first stock, you need to understand the accounts, KYC and basic documents involved.

◷ ~7 min • Beginner level
01
FIRST THING TO KNOW

You don't buy stocks directly from the stock exchange.

As a beginner, you'll normally use a SEBI-registered stock broker and the accounts connected to it.

Think of the broker as the platform that lets you place buy and sell orders in the market.

02
ACCOUNT #1

Your Demat account is where your shares live.

"Demat" basically means dematerialised — securities are held electronically instead of as physical certificates.

When you buy shares and they are delivered to you, they are held electronically in your demat account.

▦
DEMAT ACCOUNT

Your digital stock cupboard.

Holds your securities electronically.

HOLDS SHARES
03
ACCOUNT #2

Your Trading account is where you place orders.

Your trading account is used to interact with the market through your broker.

When you decide to buy or sell a stock, you use the broker's trading platform to place the order.

↗
TRADING ACCOUNT

Your market control panel.

Used to place buy and sell orders.

PLACES ORDERS

KYC

Before opening the account, you have to prove who you are.

That's where KYC — Know Your Client comes in.

It is part of the process used to verify your identity and address when opening securities-related accounts.

06
CHOOSING A BROKER

Don't choose a broker just because an ad looks cool.

Your broker is going to handle your market orders, so regulation, reliability, security, support and the actual fee structure matter.

01
Check registration

Make sure the intermediary is properly registered with the relevant regulator.

02
Understand the charges

Read the broker's current tariff sheet before opening an account.

03
Check security

Use official apps and websites and protect your passwords, PINs and OTPs.

04
Don't blindly follow influencers

A popular recommendation isn't automatically the right choice for you.
