STAGE 03 · UNDERSTANDING STOCKS

A stock isn't just a number moving on a screen. Let's understand what that number actually represents.

◷ ~7 min • Beginner level
01
THE SIMPLEST DEFINITION

A stock is a tiny piece of ownership in a company.

When you buy an equity share of a company, you become a shareholder — meaning you own a small part of that company.

The smaller your ownership percentage, the smaller your piece of the company.

IMAGINE A COMPANY
COMPANY 100% ownership
YOU
One tiny piece can still represent real ownership.
02
WHY COMPANIES ISSUE SHARES

Companies can raise money by selling ownership.

A company may issue shares to raise capital for expansion, projects, operations or other business purposes.

Investors buy those shares because they believe the company may create value in the future.

COMPANY 🏢 Raises capital
→
INVESTOR 👤 Buys shares
→
OWNERSHIP % Becomes shareholder
03
THE NUMBER YOU SEE

The stock price is simply the current market price per share.

If a stock is displayed at ₹500, that means one share is currently being quoted around ₹500 in the market.

But here's something important: a ₹500 stock isn't automatically more expensive than a ₹50 stock.

COMPANY A ₹50 Per share
VS
COMPANY B ₹500 Per share
💡 Share price alone doesn't tell you whether a company is cheap or expensive.
04
MARKET CAPITALISATION

To understand a company's size, look beyond the share price.

Market capitalisation is broadly calculated by multiplying the company's share price by the number of outstanding shares.

That's why two companies with very different share prices can still have similar overall values.

SIMPLE FORMULA
SHARE PRICE ₹100
×
SHARES 10 Lakh
=
MARKET CAP ₹100 Cr

Think of market cap as a rough way to describe the total market value of a company's outstanding shares.

05
WHAT KIND OF COMPANY?

Stocks represent businesses from completely different industries.

Before you even look at a chart, understand what the company actually does.

A bank, a software company and a food company can all have stocks — but their businesses, risks and drivers can be very different.

🏦 BANKING Financial services
💻 TECHNOLOGY Software & IT
🏭 INDUSTRIAL Manufacturing
🛒 CONSUMER Products & services
⚡ ENERGY Power & resources
💊 HEALTHCARE Pharma & healthcare
06
WHY PRICES MOVE

So why does the number keep changing?

Stock prices move because buyers and sellers continuously interact in the market.

Expectations about a company's future, earnings, news, economic conditions, sentiment and many other factors can influence demand and supply.

PRICE MOVEMENT EXPECTATIONS CHANGE
NEWS RESULTS SENTIMENT ECONOMY
07
DIVIDENDS

Some companies may share part of their profits with shareholders.

This payment is called a dividend.

But dividends are not guaranteed. Some listed companies pay dividends, while others may choose not to.

₹
POSSIBLE SHAREHOLDER BENEFIT

Dividend

A company may distribute part of its profits to eligible shareholders.

NOT GUARANTEED
08
THE BIGGEST MISUNDERSTANDING

A good company doesn't mean its stock price can only go up.

Even a real, profitable business can experience falling stock prices.

Markets react to expectations about the future, so prices can move both up and down.

STOCK ₹100
↓
MARKET PRICE ₹80
-20% Price fell by ₹20
💡
REMEMBER THIS

A stock represents ownership — but its market price can change constantly.

Understand the business first, then understand what the market is pricing it at.
